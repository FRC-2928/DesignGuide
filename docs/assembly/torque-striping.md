## When you need this

A **torque stripe** is a short line of paint drawn across a bolt head and onto the part next to it, right after the bolt is tightened. If the bolt turns, even a little, the line breaks or the two halves stop lining up. Aircraft and race-car mechanics use the same trick, often with a paint marker or a paste sold as "torque seal."

At competition you have a few minutes between matches. You can't put a wrench on every bolt. With stripes, you can look over the drivetrain in 30 seconds and only touch the bolts that moved.

## Which bolts to stripe

Stripe bolts that would hurt you if they backed out, and that you can see without taking the robot apart.

- Swerve module mounting bolts and the bolts that hold the module together
- Wheel and bevel gear bolts
- Gearbox and motor mounting bolts
- Bolts holding arms, elevators and other mechanisms to their shafts or pivots
- Bumper mounting hardware
- Set screws and shaft collars, if you can reach them

You don't need to stripe every screw on a polycarbonate cover. Spend the paint where a loose bolt ends your match.

## Steps

1. **Torque the bolt first.** A stripe on a bolt that wasn't tightened right only tells you it hasn't moved since you got it wrong. See [Torque Wrench](torque-wrench.md).
2. **Wait for threadlocker if the guide says to.** If the bolt has Loctite and the vendor says to let it cure, stripe it after the final tighten.
3. **Clean the spot.** Wipe off grease and oil with a rag. Paint won't stick to an oily surface, and a smudged stripe is useless.
4. **Shake the paint pen** and press the tip on scrap until paint flows.
5. **Draw one line** across the bolt head (or nut), across the washer if there is one, and onto the part. Make it about 1/8" wide and long enough to see from a few feet away.
6. **Let it dry** before you handle that area. Most paint markers are dry to the touch in a few minutes.
7. If a bolt has a nut on the other side, stripe the nut too. Either one can turn.

!!! tip "Photo needed"
    Take a close-up of a striped #10 bolt on one of our swerve modules: one good stripe, and one where the bolt has turned and the line is broken. A beginner learns this faster from a picture than from text.

## Inspecting stripes between matches

Do this every time the robot comes back to the pit, before anything else.

1. Look at every striped bolt. A good stripe is one straight, unbroken line.
2. If the line is broken or the two halves are offset, that bolt has moved. Don't just tighten it and move on.
3. For a moved bolt, back it out, check the threads, add threadlocker if it needs it, and re-torque it to spec.
4. Clean off the old paint with a rag or fingernail, then draw a new stripe.
5. Tell the pit lead which bolt moved. A bolt that loosens every match is a design problem, not a maintenance problem.

## Common mistakes

- Striping a bolt that was never torqued.
- Striping over grease, so the paint flakes off and every stripe looks broken.
- Painting only the bolt head and not the part next to it. Then there's nothing to compare against.
- Seeing a broken stripe, tightening the bolt, and not re-striping it.
- Drawing a huge blob instead of a thin line. A blob can hide a small turn.
- Getting paint pen on bearings or inside gear teeth.
