Keeping swerve gears lubricated so they don't wear out.

## When you need this

A **swerve module** is one corner of a swerve drivetrain. It has two motors: one spins the wheel (drive) and one turns the whole wheel to point it in a direction (steer, or azimuth). Inside are gears that see a lot of load. The most important pair is the **bevel gears**, two cone-shaped gears that turn the motion 90 degrees so a vertical shaft can spin the wheel. REV's MAXSwerve guide points out that the wheel bevel gear and pinion have high friction, so they need grease the most.

Dry gears wear fast. You'll see gray or black metal dust in the grease, hear grinding, and eventually teeth wear down or break. You grease modules:

- When you first build them
- Whenever you take a module apart (to change tread, swap a bearing, or fix something)
- As part of regular maintenance during the season

**Grease** is a thick lubricant, basically oil mixed with a thickener so it stays where you put it instead of flinging off.

## Which grease, and where

Each vendor has its own guidance. Follow the guide for the module on our robot.

| Module | Vendor says to grease | Grease named by vendor | Bolts |
|---|---|---|---|
| SDS MK4 / MK4i | All gears, after assembly | Not named, just "grease" | Loctite 242/243 on all bolts except the encoder mount bolts |
| WCP Swerve X2 | All gears, at first assembly and during maintenance | Lucas Red "N" Tacky | Vibra-Tite VC-3 on all bolts at assembly |
| REV MAXSwerve | Speed pinion (the most), steering gear, wheel bevel gear, drive spur gear | Lucas White Lithium or Lucas Red "N" Tacky #2. For a quick top-up, WD-40 Specialist White Lithium spray. | Loctite blue 242. Avoid red and green. |

## Steps

These are general steps. The vendor's assembly guide has the real order for taking your module apart.

1. **Put on nitrile gloves.** Grease is messy and hard to get off your hands and out of carpet.
2. **Open the module** following the vendor guide, far enough to reach the gears. Keep the bolts in a tray, sorted by where they came from.
3. **Look before you wipe.** Check the old grease. Clean grease is normal. Gray or black grease with metal flakes means gears are wearing. Check the teeth for chips, flat spots or cracks and show a mentor anything that looks wrong.
4. **Clean out old, dirty grease** with a rag or paper towel. If it's full of carpet fibers or metal, clean the gears fully before regreasing. Some teams soak metal parts in mineral spirits during a full teardown. Keep solvents away from bearings with rubber seals and 3D-printed parts.
5. **Apply new grease to the gear teeth.** Work it into the teeth with a gloved finger. On the bevel gears, put a bead along the teeth and spin the gears by hand to spread it. REV suggests about a pinky finger's worth on the MAXSwerve speed pinion and a moderate amount on the steering gear.
6. **Wipe off the extra.** Grease on the outside of the module collects carpet fibers and dirt. Keep it off the wheel tread and the encoder.
7. **Reassemble** using the vendor's threadlocker on each bolt. Some vendors say to use new bolts at yearly service (WCP does).
8. **Spin it by hand.** Turn the wheel and steer the module. Both should move smoothly with no grinding or tight spots.
9. **Torque and stripe** the bolts. See [Torque Wrench](torque-wrench.md) and [Torque Striping](torque-striping.md).

## Service schedule

Most vendors don't give a fixed number of hours. Here's what the vendors we checked say:

- **WCP Swerve X2**: check bolts with their bolt checklist periodically during practice and after every match at events. During general maintenance, check gears for wear, reapply grease, and make sure everything spins smoothly. Once a year, take the module apart, replace worn gears, clean everything, and rebuild with new bolts, threadlocker and fresh grease.
- **REV MAXSwerve**: reapply grease as needed.
- **SDS MK4/MK4i**: grease after assembly. The guide doesn't give an interval.

Grease is also needed any time a module is opened, for any reason.

## Common mistakes

- Using the wrong grease because it was on the shelf. Use what the vendor names, or what the team picked.
- Packing so much grease in that it squeezes out and collects carpet fibers.
- Getting grease on the wheel tread, which makes the robot slide.
- Getting grease on the steering encoder or its magnet.
- Regreasing without looking at the old grease first, and missing a gear that's wearing out.
- Reassembling without threadlocker.
- Using red Loctite on module bolts.
- Forgetting a shim or spacer on reassembly. On WCP modules, the wrong bevel gear spacer can make the gears bind.
