# Band Saw

Cutting plate and odd shapes to rough size.

!!! warning "Sign-off required"
    You must be signed off by a mentor on the band saw before you use it. Read [Shop Safety](../general/safety.md) first.

## When you need this

Our band saw is a **vertical** band saw. The blade is a long loop of thin steel with teeth on one edge, running down through a table. You hold the part flat on the table and push it into the blade. It cuts downward, into the table, so it doesn't kick the part back at you like a table saw can.

Use it for:

- Cutting plate to rough size.
- Curves, notches and odd shapes.
- Short cuts that don't fit in the [chop saw](chop-saw.md) vise.

For cutting tube and shaft to length, use the [chop saw](chop-saw.md). It clamps the part and cuts it square.

The band saw isn't accurate to the thousandth, so cut a little long (about 1/16") and finish to the line with a file, sander or the router.

The saw always has a metal-cutting blade on it. Mentors choose, set and change the blade. If the blade looks damaged, is missing teeth or keeps wandering, stop and tell a mentor.

## Dust collection

The band saw is connected to the shop vacuum system. Have the vacuum pulling the whole time you cut. It keeps chips off the table and out of the air, and keeps the lower wheel housing from filling up with chips.

1. Turn on the shop vacuum system.
2. Open the blast gate (the slide valve on the duct) at the band saw.
3. Close the gates at machines nobody is using, so the band saw gets full suction.
4. When you're done, close the band saw gate. Turn the vacuum off if no one else is using it.

## Steps

1. Mark your cut line with a scribe or marker. Cut on the waste side of the line.
2. With the saw off, set the upper blade guide just above the part, about 1/4" or less. This covers the blade and supports it so it doesn't twist.
3. Turn on the vacuum and open the band saw's blast gate (see [Dust collection](#dust-collection)).
4. Put on safety glasses. No gloves. Tie back hair.
5. Turn the saw on and let it reach full speed.
6. Hold the part flat on the table. Feed it into the blade with steady, light pressure. Let the teeth do the cutting.
7. Keep your fingers to the sides of the blade, never in line with it. When your hand gets within a few inches of the blade, switch to a push stick.
8. For curves, cut relief cuts first (straight cuts from the edge to the curve) so the waste falls away in small pieces. Don't twist the blade around a tight turn.
9. Finish the cut, then pull the part back only if the blade is clear. Otherwise turn the saw off and wait for the blade to stop.
10. Turn the saw off. Brush chips off the table once the blade has stopped. Close the blast gate.

## Common mistakes

- Cutting without the vacuum on, or with the gate closed.
- Leaving the upper blade guide raised high above the part.
- Putting fingers in line with the blade.
- Forcing the feed. The blade wanders and the cut ends up crooked.
- Reaching in to clear a scrap while the blade is still moving.
- Cutting exactly on the line, so the part ends up short after cleanup.
