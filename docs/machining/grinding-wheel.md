# Grinding Wheel

Shaping and cleaning up steel parts and tools.

!!! warning "Sign-off required"
    You must be signed off by a mentor on the grinder before you use it. Read [Shop Safety](../general/safety.md) first.

## When you need this

The bench grinder has two abrasive wheels spinning at high speed. You hold steel against the front of a wheel to grind it down. We use it to:

- Round over or shorten the cut end of a steel bolt or threaded rod.
- Clean up rough edges on steel parts.
- Sharpen a scribe or center punch.

It's one of the more dangerous tools in the shop. The wheel spins fast enough that a cracked wheel can break apart and throw pieces, and it throws a constant shower of hot sparks and grit.

## What not to grind

**Never grind aluminum, brass or plastic on the grinding wheel.** These are only for steel.

Why:

- **Soft metals clog the wheel.** Aluminum smears into the pores of the wheel, which is called **loading**. A loaded wheel stops cutting, heats up and can crack. A cracked wheel can come apart at full speed.
- **Plastic melts** onto the wheel and does the same damage.

For aluminum, use a file, a belt sander with a belt for aluminum, or a deburring tool instead. See [Deburring](deburring.md).

## Setup and tool rest gap

Check these every time, with the grinder off and unplugged:

1. **Tool rest gap: 1/8" or less.** The **tool rest** is the small shelf in front of the wheel. The gap between it and the wheel must be no more than 1/8". If the gap is bigger, a small part can get dragged into it and jam between the rest and the wheel. That can break the wheel or pull your fingers in.
2. **Wheel condition.** Look for chips, cracks or a wheel that looks loaded with metal. Don't use it if anything looks wrong. Tell a mentor.
3. **Eye shields** are in place and clean.

As the wheel wears down, the gaps grow, so they need adjusting. Ask a mentor to adjust the rest or guard, or to replace a wheel. A new wheel has to be checked and run in before anyone uses it.

## Steps

1. Put on safety glasses **and** a face shield. No gloves. Tie back hair, roll up sleeves.
2. Check the gaps and the wheel as above.
3. Stand to the side of the wheel, not in front of it, and switch it on. If a wheel is going to break, it usually happens at startup. Let it run up to full speed.
4. Rest the part on the tool rest. Hold it firmly with both hands.
5. Slide it gently forward on the rest until it touches the front face of the wheel. Never grind on the side of a straight wheel, since side pressure can crack it.
6. Move the part slowly side to side across the wheel face so the wheel wears evenly.
7. Steel gets hot fast. Dip it in the water cup often. If it's too hot to hold, it's too hot. Don't use gloves to hold it.
8. For very small parts, hold them in locking pliers instead of your fingers.
9. Turn the grinder off and wait for it to stop before you walk away.

## Common mistakes

- Grinding aluminum. This is the big one.
- Wearing gloves because the part is hot. Cool the part in water instead.
- Grinding on the side of the wheel.
- Standing in front of the wheel when switching it on.
- Pushing hard. The wheel does the work, and pressure heats the part and the wheel.
