# Chop Saw

Cutting tube and shaft to rough length.

!!! warning "Sign-off required"
    You must be signed off by a mentor on the chop saw before you use it. Read [Shop Safety](../general/safety.md) first.

## When you need this

The metal chop saw cuts stock to rough length before final machining. We mostly use it for:

- Aluminum box tube and round tube.
- Hex and round shaft.

The part is clamped in a vise, and you pull a spinning circular blade down through it. That gives a straight, square cut, and it's much quicker than the [band saw](band-saw.md) for cutting a lot of tube to length. For plate, curves and notches, use the band saw instead.

Like the band saw, it's a rough cut. Cut about 1/16" long and finish to length on the lathe, router or with a file.

The saw always has a metal-cutting blade on it. Mentors choose and change the blade.

## Dust collection

The chop saw is connected to the shop vacuum system. Have the vacuum pulling the whole time you cut. It catches most of the chips the blade throws.

1. Turn on the shop vacuum system.
2. Open the blast gate (the slide valve on the duct) at the chop saw.
3. Close the gates at machines nobody is using, so the chop saw gets full suction.
4. When you're done, close the chop saw gate. Turn the vacuum off if no one else is using it.

## Steps

1. Mark your cut line. Cut on the waste side of the line.
2. With the saw switched off, look the blade over and check that the blade guard springs back down over it on its own. If the blade is missing teeth or the guard sticks, don't use the saw. Tell a mentor.
3. Clamp the part tightly in the vise, with the cut line lined up with the blade. Never hold the part by hand. Support long stock at the far end so it stays level.
4. Leave the offcut (the piece on the far side of the blade) free to fall away. Don't clamp both ends tight against the blade, or the blade can bind.
5. Turn on the vacuum and open the chop saw's blast gate (see [Dust collection](#dust-collection)).
6. Put on safety glasses and hearing protection. A face shield is a good idea too. No gloves. Tie back hair.
7. Keep your hands and arms out of the blade's path. Stand a little to the side of the blade, not directly behind it.
8. Pull the trigger and let the blade reach full speed **before** it touches the part.
9. Lower the blade with slow, steady pressure. Let the teeth cut. Slow down as you break through the bottom of the tube.
10. Release the trigger and **keep the blade down** until it stops spinning. Raising a spinning blade can catch the offcut and throw it.
11. Raise the blade once it has stopped. The cut ends and chips are hot and sharp. Let them cool before you touch them.
12. Deburr the cut ends. See [Deburring](deburring.md).
13. Brush chips off the vise and saw once the blade has stopped. Close the blast gate.

## Common mistakes

- Cutting without the vacuum on, or with the gate closed.
- Holding the part by hand instead of clamping it.
- Starting the cut before the blade is at full speed.
- Forcing the blade down. It heats up, wanders, and leaves a rough, crooked cut.
- Raising the blade while it's still spinning.
- Reaching for the offcut before the blade stops.
- Cutting very short pieces that can't be clamped well. Leave the stock long and cut the short piece off the end.
