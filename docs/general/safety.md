# Shop Safety

Read this before you use any tool or machine in the shop. Every machine page in this guide assumes you know it.

Most shop injuries aren't dramatic. They're a cut from a burr, a chip in the eye, or a sleeve caught in a drill press. Nearly all of them come from skipping something on this page.

## Before you start

### Safety glasses

Wear safety glasses the whole time you're in the shop, not just when you're running a machine. The person next to you might be drilling, and chips don't care whose eyes they land in.

- Regular glasses aren't enough. Wear safety glasses over them or use prescription safety glasses with side shields.
- Glasses must be marked **Z87** (the ANSI standard for impact protection). It's printed on the frame.
- Put on a face shield over your glasses for the grinder and any time a mentor tells you to.

### Clothing, hair and jewelry

Rotating machines grab anything loose and pull it in faster than you can react.

- Tie back long hair. Tuck it in if it can still swing forward.
- Take off rings, bracelets, watches and necklaces.
- Roll up long sleeves above the elbow, or wear short sleeves. No hoodie strings.
- Wear closed-toe shoes. Dropped stock and chips are sharp and heavy.
- **No gloves on rotating machines** (drill press, lathe, router, grinder). A glove can get caught and pull your hand in. Gloves are fine for handling sharp stock and for cleanup with the machine off.

## Machine rules

1. **Get signed off first.** You need a mentor's sign-off before you use any machine on your own. Reading the guide page is part of that, not a replacement for it.
2. **Know where the stop is.** Before you turn a machine on, find its off switch or E-stop (emergency stop button) and make sure you can reach it.
3. **Clamp the work.** Never hold a part by hand on the drill press or the router. If it spins, it hits you.
4. **Remove keys and wrenches.** Take the chuck key out of the chuck every time, before you let go of it.
5. **Clear chips with a brush,** never your hand or compressed air at your face. Chips are sharp and hot.
6. **Stay with a running machine.** Don't walk away from it until it has fully stopped.
7. **One person runs the machine.** Everyone else stays out of the way and doesn't distract them.
8. **Clean up when you're done.** Sweep chips, put tools back, and leave the machine the way you found it.

!!! warning "If something feels wrong, stop"
    A strange noise, smoke, a part that's slipping, or a tool that's chattering means stop the machine and ask a mentor. Nobody will be upset that you stopped. They will be upset if you didn't.

## Battery and electrical safety

An FRC battery can deliver hundreds of amps into a short circuit. That's enough to weld a wrench to a terminal or start a fire.

- Turn off the main breaker before working on robot wiring.
- Never let a metal tool touch both battery terminals. Keep terminal covers on spare batteries.
- Don't carry batteries by the wires. Carry them by the handle or the case.
- A battery that's swollen, cracked, leaking or hot doesn't get used or charged. Tell a mentor.
- Before you enable the robot, say "enabling" out loud and make sure nobody's hands are in it.

More on this is in [Electrical](../electrical/index.md).

## If someone gets hurt

1. Stop the machine. Hit the E-stop if you need to.
2. Get a mentor right away, even for something that seems small.
3. For a cut, apply pressure with clean gauze from the first aid kit.
4. For something in the eye, don't rub it. Use the eyewash station and get a mentor.
5. For anything serious, call 911 first, then get a mentor.

Every injury gets reported to a mentor, even a small one, so we can fix whatever caused it.
